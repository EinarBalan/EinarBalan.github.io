# on self reference
the liar's paradox, and nathan fielder | 10/29/2026 | X min

---

you've probably heard of the liar's paradox. maybe not the name, but you've almost certainly been exposed to it in one form or another. 

> *"This sentence is false."*

it's pretty clear why the sentence is problematic. if we trust it to be false, then the sentence becomes true, but if we take it to be true then it becomes false, and so on, recursing infinitely back and forth. likewise if we start under the assumption that it's true. basically baby's first paradox.

but it does hint at some of the most satisfying and beautiful truths and ambiguities in the world. in language, in media, in art, in science, and in our everyday relationships. we see it everywhere.

## why am I thinking about this

just like literally everyone else in the world, I recently watched the nathan fielder elizabeth holmes<sup>1</sup> doc. and yes it is as good as everyone is saying and yes you should absolutely watch it, but be warned that is is an extremely uncomfortable experience and if you're like me, you will probably hate every second of it. 

I won't get into it because at this point I feel that everything that needed to be said about it has been said and honestly I'm getting really bored of hearing people debate their different interpretations and the ambiguity of it all.<sup>2</sup>

what I've come to realize more than anything in the fallout of this is that people fucking love ambiguity. there are so many examples. when we can't see the monster under the bed, we're absolutely terrified. when manic pixie dream girl ignores our texts, we idealize her. when the message of a movie is unclear, we hail it as a masterpiece.

ambiguity leaves space for more. the possibility that something is more insightful than it actually is. and we fall for it every time. and we will continue to do so because we fucking love it. as much as we hate to admit it, we love not knowing because it lets us insert ourselves, and what's better than us?

anyways. earlier I was reading through [Derek Thompson's thoughts]((https://www.derekthompson.org/p/what-is-you-can-see-everything-actually?r=3dkp&utm_medium=ios&triedRedirect=true#footnote-anchor-3)) on You Can See Everything, and I stumbled across this part:

> *"The existential philosopher Soren Kierkegaard once wrote that 'the self is a relation that relates itself to itself.' This nearly sounds like nonsense. It’s also a lovely description of Fielder’s documentary style. He loves asking his subjects to play themselves and watch themselves be played, always searching for a way to depict the act of self-awareness."*

after getting over the initial slap in the face of how pretentious it sounded, I gave it some more thought. and it made me realize how deeply interconnected self reference and ambiguity are. and in turn made me want to reflect on various self referential things that I have come to appreciate in my own life. so here I am.
