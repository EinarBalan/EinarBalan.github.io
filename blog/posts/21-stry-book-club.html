book club

on the table was an arrangement of chocolate chip cookies, coffee cake, a carved cantelope covered in caramel confit, and a colorful bouquet of clovers and carnations.

to this point, it had been left mostly untouched, much to the disappointment of Linda S. she thought surely at least Linda B. would have gone for a cookie by now. or maybe Linda F.?

but no. it was a bit hurtful if we're perfectly honest because she had gone to great lengths to make this as pleasant of a meeting as it could possibly be. after the last one, she knew she only had one more chance, and what better way to put people in a good mood?

* * *

the idea for the Linda club had come to her in a dream a few months back. upon waking up, she had been utterly convinced that it was a sign from god, and got to work.

retirement had been lonely for Linda S. her husband was long gone, and there were no kids in the picture either. most days she woke up alone, had brunch alone, went for a walk alone, read her book alone, went to the pool alone, and went to bed alone.

she'd had enough of being alone.

* * *

she was quite proud of the first poster. she had placed it on her community bulletin to the right of an ad for a "classics" book club and to the left of a yellow index card advertising free dog walking, in the hopes that hers would attract a similar audience.

"Is your name Linda?"* it read. "Mine too! Us Lindas need to stick together. Come to 1500 I St. on Saturday mornings and meet the Lindas of Modesto!"

it hadn't started as a book club, but the Lindas promptly noticed the common interest, and so a book club it became. they agreed to meet on the second Saturday of each month and Linda S. benevolently proposed a democractic system for deciding on a book.

in this way, they had landed first on Wuthering Heights, then Little Women, and now Frankenstein (given the season).

* * *

Linda S. greeted everyone with a smile as they walked in, glancing intermittently between her cookie platter and Linda B., who had been one of the first to arrive. Linda S. was wearing a tasteful green and orange floral print blouse and jeans, an outfit designed to look effortless, but which she had secretly stewed over for hours the night before.

"ok!" she said enthusiastically. "seems like most everyone is here."

she glanced at the empty seat assigned to Linda W., who had been at least ten minutes late to every meeting so far.

"let's get started!" she pulled out her notebook, onto which she'd jotted down a couple questions.

"so what did we think? did we enjoy Frankenstein? I know it's a bit different than what we normally read."

"I thought it was delightful, just beautifully put together, that Mary Shelley really knows what she's doing." Linda H. was always the first to speak up. she was the youngest of the group at 32, and by far the most energetic. she had dirty blonde hair, painfully maintained eyebrows, and was wearing a plain white tank top, long khaki pants, with her signature comically large, dark framed sunglasses pushed up into her hair like a headband.

"I really enjoyed the prose," continued H. "like, it was shockingly readable. I think I had read this one way back in like freshman year of high school or something and I absolutely hated it then because I had literally no idea what was going on the entire time, and if I'm being honest I was kinda dreading reading it this month, but really pleasantly surprised!! to me it was a story less about monsters and mad scientists and moreso about the danger of ambition." she paused and looked around to see if people were impressed. "…like an Icarus moment, you know what I mean? like Dr. Frankenstein really flew too close to the sun with this one. he had ambition and, you know, that's great and all but I think Mary Shelley was really trying to warn us to not get too carried away, you know? I don't know. that's what I got from it. maybe I'm wrong, I don't know. I could be wrong, I guess. but I don't think I am. ambition guys! I'm ambitious. but I know when to stop flying, you know what I mean?? Dr. Frankenstein didn't and we saw what happened to him."

all the Lindas nodded their heads in approval. sensing H was done, S jumped in. "yes! 100% yes. I am so on board with that. I actually was gonna ask about themes. how'd you know? jumped right ahead. does anyone else want to share thoughts on theme?"

there was silence for a while, until Linda T. began shifting around nervously in her seat. voice trembling, she said, "yeah H…. that's like… really well said. that's kind of what I wanted to say… too… so I agree." Linda T. was closer to Linda S.'s age, though she had never disclosed it. she had a red pair of reading glasses, the kind with a chain that wrapped around the back of your head and was the only Linda aside from S to touch any of the snacks, now sitting with a plate of canteloupe in front of her. "I think… to me… what also stuck out was… the description of… the monster… particularly towards the second act… I liked…"

before T could finish, Linda W. walked in holding hands with a man none of them had ever seen before who looked to be about 30 years younger, wearing a silly grin on his face. they seemed to have come from the pool, and were both still dripping a bit.

"hi everyone. sorry I'm late. this is Charles," said W.

everyone looked at her in disbelief. Linda H. looked particularly annoyed. "Charles??" she muttered.

sensing the group's uncertainty, Linda W. continued, "well okay, I guess I should say, yeah I know we're Lindas, right? and you might be like well what business does a Charles have in Linda club?? but listen, okay, he's cool. he's fun. he's sexy! and guess what? he IS actually a Linda. Charles Linden. that's his name. can you believe it?? Linden. what are the odds? met him earlier this week! we're in love!"

as she spoke, the smile fell from Linden's face and he began to look around apologetically, clearly having not been briefed on the particulars of the event.

Linda S., eager to get back on track, stood up and began looking for a new chair for Charles saying "oh great! a new member. the more the merrier. welcome Linden. let me find—"

"ARE YOU KIDDING ME??" Linda H. "we're just going to let her do this?? not only is she late every meeting, not only is she dripping wet, but she also brought a Charles to Linda club! and we're just going to let that happen? who are we if we let Charles in? just another book club? no! we're the Linda club. we have principles. sorry Charles. you seem like a nice guy. great smile. really. but this is not the time. we have standards!"

"didn't you hear me?" Linda W. "he IS a Linda. Charles LINDEN. LIN. DEN." she motioned side to side with each syllable. "and anyways he's more suited to join than half the Lindas here. he's way more fun than most of you! certainly more fun than you H!" she pointed at H, angrily.

"LINDEN?" H. "since when is LINDEN, Linda? completely different. and I don't care how fun he is (again you seem like a nice boy Charles), he doesn't belong here! and you know what, maybe you don't either!"

at this, Linda W. steps forward and slaps Linda H. with the full force of her palm. Linda H's sunglasses and Linda W's water droplets fly everywhere, getting all over the cookie and cake arrangement.

for a moment, everyone lies motionless in disbelief. H stares at W, mouth agape, hand drawn up to the red mark W had left behind on her face. she looks to Linda S. who quickly shakes her head disapprovingly, then looks to Linda T. who looks smaller than she has ever looked before, then back to W. and she lets out a bellowing scream, and reaches to grab the side of W's bob haircut, and pulls with all her force.

W's wig slides right off, and her bald cap is exposed. Linden looks at her in disgust and in terror, then starts for the door.

"NO!" W points at Linden. "YOU BELONG HERE. YOU ARE A LINDA. STAY." and she winds up and punches H square in the jaw. a tooth flies out of her mouth and lands on the top of the coffee cake. all the Lindas rush out of their seats, to pull them apart. Linden begins bawling, but obeys, staying close behind W.

in the chaos, Linda S. doesn't move. she looks up at the ceiling, then to Linda H's tooth on the coffee cake and the grimy wet spots all over the cookies.

with a stare a thousand miles long, she reaches over to one of the cookies and takes a bite.
