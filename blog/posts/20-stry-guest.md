# my guest
1625 words | 11/24/2026 

----

Andrew Baron was in the unfortunate habit of never locking his door. 

if you asked him about it he'd probably sit up a little straighter, widen his eyes, then start his well practiced rant about the goodness of humanity, inching closer to you with every word, exclaiming breathlessly that trust has to start somewhere,  and culminating with a signature finger in your face — "I can trust YOU, can't I? can't I... CAN'T I?!" 

the reality, of course, was that he was incapable of remembering which of his numerous keys opened the front door, and the last thing he wanted to do after a long day of work was look for it. 

needless to say, he much preferred the human goodness explanation.

<p align="center">* * *</p>

one tuesday afternoon in fall, Andrew was on his way home a bit earlier than usual. 

it was a particularly beautiful day. the whole ride all he could think about were the turning leaves on the trees, the sound of children laughing in the distance, and the deep blue, partially cloudy sky. he had a hike planned on the coming weekend with a friend he had met at the dog park (he didn't have a dog) and if the weather was anything like today's, he was sure it would be a wonderful time.

twenty minutes later, Andrew pulled into his driveway. he lived in the kind of neighborhood that parents dream of, kids love, and that teenagers can't wait to leave. the kind where you know all your neighbors and they all know you, and you often get together on weekends.

Andrew's neighbors all quite respected him, despite him being the only bachelor in the area. normally that would have been difficult to overcome, but given how fervently he decorated his house every holiday season, they were willing to look past it. with each year, he outdid himself, buying an enormous inflatable turkey for Thanksgiving or an obnoxiously bright, LED laden reindeer for Christmas.

this year his proudest purchase was a groundbreaking, 20 foot tall, acrylic Jack Skellington statue that spoke intermittently, delivering such gems as "This year, Christmas will be ours!" and "I AM THE PUMPKIN KING!!" and of course he couldn't not get the accompanying ghost dog for $50 extra.

to Andrew's surprise as he slammed his car door shut, he noticed that Zero the dog was sitting knocked over on its back beside Jack. 

"*hm, must have been the wind,*" thought Andrew. he picked it up and drove the transparent stake back into the ground where he had placed it earlier, then started back down his driveway toward the garage door.

as he got closer, he noticed it had been left slightly ajar. he looked at it, shrugged, and stepped inside.

<p align="center">* * *</p>

immediately he was hit with the sickly sweet mix of an unfamiliar B.O. and what he identified as a Calvin Klein Eau de Parfum. looking around, he saw mud tracks crossing the foyer toward the living room. there was a puddle of some unidentified liquid near the shoe rack as well as a single muddy Air Force 1 sneaker which was definitely not his.

plugging his nose, he ventured onward toward the living room. he remembered that he had a bat in a closet beside the garage door, but opted to leave it behind. by this point, he became keenly aware of his own breath, to the point where it was no longer automatic, so he made an effort to take deep, quiet breaths through his nose in order to stay calm.

stepping through the doorway, he saw a large elderly white man with a single shoe resting on his couch. he was wearing a white tank top which exposed his thin, swollen arms rife with varicose veins, a nice pair of khakis, and a woven leather belt with a steel buckle. in a perfect outline surrounding him, there was a newly added dark wet stain on the couch fabric. 

the man had his eyes open, but seemed not to notice Andrew as he stepped in. Andrew waited a second, taking it all in, unsure of how best to handle the situation. he looked at the man with empathy rather than fear, and was worried that he'd lost his way.

"hello?" said Andrew.

the man didn't reply. Andrew took a step closer.

"hello?" he repeated.

this time the man shot to attention. he seemed startled for a moment, then relaxed back into his stain.

"haven't you ever heard of knocking?" the man said. "you scared me!"

Andrew looked back at the doorway, which didn't have a door, and then at the man again.

"oh, sorry about that," said Andrew.

"oh no worries. sometimes I forget too. it's easy to forget. you know, you remind me of my son. he's about your age."

"how old is he?"

"I think he just turned 34."

"oh well, I'm not quite there yet. I'm 25."

"eh 25, 34. it's all the same. just wait til you're 75 like me. then you can talk. my son's a great guy though. you would like him. my youngest I mean. the oldest is an asshole. don't even get me started. my youngest does consulting at a pharmaceutical company out in Texas. been there a few years now. says he reallly likes it. and they pay well! it's a great job. it really is. he's far better off than I was at his age. which is what you want! it's great. really great. oh he's so funny. there's a funny story I like to tell people." he paused for a few moments and looked at Andrew expectantly.

Andrew stayed silent and stared back.

"yeah, so when my youngest first started school, he didn't really know what he wanted to study right. so he was trying all kinds of things. you name it, he tried it. he started off with philosophy. philosophy of science or whatever. or maybe bachelor of science? I never understood any of that stuff. anyways the philosophy stuff proved to be way too difficult for him. he was never the best reader and they had him reading 2 books a week. can you believe that! I haven't read 2 books in the last 5 years! and so he dropped that and for a while was just taking classes aimlessly. and I told him, I said 'Johnny, listen. you need to pick something. how about like computer stuff! everyone needs computer stuff. you'll be in high demand,' and of course he just brushed me off and said he hated computers and started studying like business or something along those lines instead. anyways two years later, he joins a computer club! and he got really into computer stuff. says he really enjoyed IU or something? you know IU right? yeah he loved IU. and the whole time I was laughing to myself because WHAT DO YOU KNOW, daddy does know best. I could've said I told you so but I never did. and now he does IU work at that pharma company in Texas. and he loves it. and yeah I just love thinking about that story, just love it."

Andrew managed a weak chuckle, and a smile. "that's a great story," he said.

"...so that's a funny story I like to tell people," the man continued, cutting Andrew off before he could say anything else. "everyone loves that story. I love that story. ugh but I guess if I told you about my youngest I should tell you about my oldest. he's a doctor. well, he's going to be a doctor. he thinks he's so great. saving lives and shit. well I made him. he came out of me! so where does that put me. where's my pat on the back? he's a boxer, actually. do you like sports?"

"oh yeah I play—"

"my son's a boxer. like I said. I never understood why. but he's a big dude. way bigger than me. who knows where he got those genes, maybe he's not mine! HA! but yeah he'll punch a dude in the face. I'm pretty athletic too, though. you know Charles Barkley. and Shaq, right? yeah well I picked Kenny Thomas's pocket once. you know Kenny Thomas. I went to school with him and we played pick up and I was so nervous. they split up the courts by skill level so I was on Kenny's court. and I picked his pocket. and you know what! he came up to me after and congratulated me! said I was one of the best he'd ever seen. oh by the way my friend lives next door. he owns a few houses here actually. it's so nostalgic being here. but yeah just in case you were wondering. anyways so yeah that's a funny story. people love that story. picked his pocket..."

the man stopped talking for a while, seemingly out of things to say. his eyes darted back and forth between Andrew and his living room. the TV was on but muted, tuned to SportsCenter. there was a college football game on.

"...so what do you do?" the man asked Andrew.

Andrew was strangely eager to impress the man. "well actually, it's funny you bring up your son because I'm—"

"OH NO!!!!!!!!!!" the man turned away from Andrew and focused his attention onto the football game. "FUCK YOU! FUCKING PIECE OF... NO!!!!!". the man stood up, took his remaining shoe off, and threw it at the TV screen. then he walked toward the doorway where Andrew was standing, shook his hand on the way out and said "hey, good talking to you man. nice to meet you." 

and then he left.
